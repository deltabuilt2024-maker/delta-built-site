Oil Spill Contingency Plans

Self-plans for coastal and offshore facilities to prepare for and respond to oil and harmful substance spills, kept up to date to NCEC requirements.

Request this serviceRegulatory requirements

When you need it

For ports, marinas, fuel loading terminals, offshore platforms and coastal desalination and power plants, and whenever NCEC requests it.

What you receive

  1. 01

    A self-plan ready for approval

  2. 02

    Spill and response scenarios

  3. 03

    A training and exercise programme

The service includes

  • Spill risk assessment and plan scope
  • Response tiers and escalation
  • Resources, equipment and staff
  • Reporting and communication
  • Training and exercises

Regulatory requirements

NCEC requirements

Reference authority

National Center for Environmental Compliance (NCEC)

Facilities must prepare a self-plan for preparedness and response approved by NCEC; NCEC's guide sets the content of plans for oil and harmful substance spills.

  • The facility plan is Tier 1 of three response tiers, approved by the national authority; response escalates to Tier 2 when an incident exceeds the facility's means.

    Oil spill self-plans guide — pp. 11–12

  • The plan is endorsed by the facility's reference authority if any, then submitted to NCEC; it is reviewed at least every two years and changed only with NCEC approval.

    Oil spill self-plans guide — p. 14

  • The plan scope follows the spill risks expected within the facility and potential external threats.

    Oil spill self-plans guide — p. 16

  • Notify NCEC immediately of any oil or harmful substance spill in the marine or coastal environment and contain it.

    Marine & Coastal Regulation — Art. 4, First (9) · p. 11

  • If the plan uses dispersants: when they are used, their composition, target locations, expected effects and how these are handled.

    Environmental Emergencies Regulation — Art. 4 (6) · pp. 8–9

A summary for guidance with page numbers in the official documents; the binding text is the document issued by the authority, which may be updated.

How we deliver it

How we deliver

  1. 1

    Scoping

    We define the activity, its area of influence, sensitive habitats and regulatory requirements.

  2. 2

    Field surveys

    We collect water, sediment, habitat and marine life data using documented methods.

  3. 3

    Modelling and analysis

    We use numerical models to predict impacts and compare them with applicable standards.

  4. 4

    Study and plans

    We prepare the study and the management, monitoring and response plans to NCEC requirements.

  5. 5

    Follow-up

    We follow the review with the authorities and run the monitoring programmes.
